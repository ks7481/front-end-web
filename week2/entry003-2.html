Entry 003: The Locksmith

Set of Keys, Three, on a Ring Shaped Like a Fish. Route 14, Northbound.

Page 2 of 3

We took Key C to a locksmith two streets over, a man who has been cutting keys since before the depot was rebuilt. He didn't need the magnifying glass. He turned it over once and put it down on the counter, gently.

"That's not a house key," he said. "That's a locker key. Coin-return, the kind they put in bus stations. They stopped making the blanks decades back."

He told us two other things, and we wrote both down before leaving.

The first: the blackening isn't age, or tarnish. It is soot. The key has been through smoke, or very near it, and the residue has worked into the grooves so deeply that no cleaning has shifted it.

The second: he asked which terminus it came from. We said we didn't know. He looked at us for a while and then said the old Route 14 used to run two stops further north than it does now, to a depot on Marrow Lane, and that Marrow Lane had the last bank of coin lockers in the city. He said this as though he was mentioning a dead relative.

We looked at the current timetable when we got back. Route 14 ends at the depot. There is no Marrow Lane on it, and none on any other route. It isn't on the council's stop list, and it isn't in the archive of retired stops, which we found and read through twice. It is as if the stop had been taken out of the record by a very steady hand.

Before we left, the locksmith looked at Key B and said it was cut within the last year. He looked at Key A and said it was older than either of us. "Somebody keeps adding to this ring," he said. "Somebody's been keeping something for a long time."

Key C
Identified: coin-locker key, Marrow Lane Terminus. Smoke-damaged.
Marrow Lane
Removed from all current records.
