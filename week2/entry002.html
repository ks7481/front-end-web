Entry 002: What Happened

A Paperback, Water-Damaged, Missing Its Cover. Route 14, Northbound.

It sat in the rain tray under seat 22 for most of a week before anyone noticed it. By then the first forty pages had swollen together into something more like cardboard, and no amount of drying separated them again.

We tried to identify the title. No spine, no cover, no copyright page — just the block of surviving text, chapters 6 through 19, and the underlined sentence on page 118. We searched libraries and secondhandshops with that sentence typed into the counter computer. Nothing matched exactly, which either means the edition is rare, or the sentence was never printed at all — that someone wrote it into the margin themselves, in the same blue pen, and we've been reading it as the author's ever since.

The story, what's left of it, follows a woman on a long train journey across a country that is never named. She is going, we think, home, though it becomes less certain by the last surviving chapter whether home is a place she is returning to or one she invented to have a reason to keep moving.

One more thing, which we noticed only on the third careful reading. The woman's destination is named several times across the surviving chapters. Every time, the name has been inked out by hand — a neat, flat bar of the same blue pen, pressed hard enough to dent the page behind it. Whoever did this was thorough. They missed exactly one instance, in chapter 17, where the bar slips and leaves the last two letters showing. They read: ne.

We don't know if she arrives. We don't know who was reading this on Route 14, or whether they finished it, or whether losing it here was an accident at all.

Status
Unclaimed. Moved to permanent archive.
Note
The underlined sentence has not been reproduced here. Some things stay in the book they were written in.
