Entry 003: Marrow Lane

Set of Keys, Three, on a Ring Shaped Like a Fish. Route 14, Northbound.

Page 3 of 3

We went on a Sunday, when the depot is quiet. The old Marrow Lane terminus is still there, more or less. It is a low brick building behind a fence with a padlock so rusted it has become part of the fence. Someone had been through the gap at the corner recently; the weeds were bent flat in a line.

The waiting hall had lost its roof at one end, and the far wall was black from floor to ceiling. Along the back stood the lockers: two rows of forty, each door a dull olive green under the soot. Most were hanging open. Some had been forced. A few were still shut, and one of those was numbered 14.

Key C went in as if it had been cut that morning. It turned a quarter and stopped. We felt the lock give, and we took our hand away.

We did not open it. We have discussed this at length since. There is a rule at the counter that we don't take from a locker anything that hasn't been signed for, and there is another rule, unwritten and unspoken, that says when a door has waited this long you don't get to be who decides when it opens.

We relocked it and left. On the way out we noticed the fence had a new chain on the inside of the gap, hung loosely, not fastened. Someone else has a key too.

There was one more thing, which we almost missed because we were looking at the lock. Low on the bottom edge of the door, at about the height of a kneeling adult, something had been scratched into the paint with a key. We looked at it for a long time.

Status
Keys A, B, C: unclaimed. Locker 14, Marrow Lane: unopened.
Note
The fish stays with the keys. We don't know why that felt important.
