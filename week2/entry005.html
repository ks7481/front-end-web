Entry 005: What Happened

A Paper Transfer Ticket, Punched, Stamped With a Date That Hasn't Happened Yet. Route 14, Northbound.

Page 1 of 2

The driver found it while counting the fare box at the end of a shift. It was folded once, neatly, and sitting between a two-pound coin and a button. The paper is the old cream stock the depot stopped printing before most of us started here.

The stamp is clear and correct in every way but one. It reads 6:40am, and the date it gives is next Tuesday.

Transfer tickets are stamped by the machine on boarding. The machine only knows the present time. We had it checked, and it is set correctly, and its log shows no ticket of this kind issued at any time this month. The paper, the ink and the stamp all point to a ticket that has not been sold yet.

Then there is the punch. Along the bottom edge, where a conductor would once have marked the stop of alighting, someone has punched a single hole beside a printed list of stops. The list is the old list. The hole is not beside any name on it. It sits below the last one, in the margin, next to a space where the paper has been left deliberately blank. A hand has written into that space, in blue pen, three words:

Marrow Lane. Northbound.

We have read that pen before.

Status
Unclaimed. Not lost, according to the paperwork. Not yet.
