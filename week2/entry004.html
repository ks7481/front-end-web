Entry 004: What Happened

A Voice Memo, 0:14 Seconds, Unlabelled. Route 14, Northbound.

Page 1 of 2

The phone was handed in on a Wednesday, found down the side of a seat, screen cracked, battery at four percent. A woman came for it the following week. She described the case, the wallpaper, the lock code, and she was right about all three, so it was returned without any trouble.

Before she took it we did what we always do and offered to list what was on the device. Photos, messages, that sort of thing. She waved most of it away, and then stopped at a single line in the file list. A voice memo, fourteen seconds long, title left blank.

"That isn't mine," she said. Flatly, without surprise.

We asked if she wanted it deleted. She thought about it for a moment and said no, she'd rather we kept it. She wouldn't say why. She signed for the phone and left, and we have the recording, which is why it's here.

We can tell you what the file's information says. It was created at 6:40am. The date is the Tuesday of Entry 001. It was not made on the phone it was found on; the phone's own records show it coming from somewhere else, over a short-range connection, at the exact moment its owner says she was asleep.

One more thing. While she waited, she looked at the box on the counter, the one where the glove from Entry 001 sits until the paperwork catches up. She looked at it for longer than people typically look at a box. Then she asked whether we'd had any keys handed in, and before we could answer, she said, "No. Don't tell me. It's fine."

Status
Unclaimed. Discarded by the phone's owner.
