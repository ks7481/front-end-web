Entry 003: What Happened

Set of Keys, Three, on a Ring Shaped Like a Fish. Route 14, Northbound.

Page 1 of 3

The Fish

They came in with the last run of the night, in the fare tray, under a scatter of dimes. The driver assumed they were his own until he checked his pockets and found his own set exactly where it should be.

The ring is a small enamelled fish, blue fading to grey, with a chip out of the tail where the paint has worn to bare metal. It is the kind of thing one would buy at a seaside stall, or be given as a joke, or given as something that was not a joke at all. The fish's eye is a single pinprick of black. Under a magnifying glass it is a hole, drilled through, and there is nothing behind it.

Key A is brass, and worn smooth across the bow by decades of the same thumb. Key B is silver and cut much more recently; the edges are still sharp. Keys A and B are the ordinary kind. A front door, and then perhaps a second front door. We didn't try to guess whose.

Key C is the problem. It is short, thick in the shank, and blackened, and it sits heavier in the palm than it looks. We tried it in every lock in this building. Filing cabinets, the cash drawer, the cupboard under the stairs, the back door, the lost-and-found lockers themselves. Nothing. It doesn't even reach in far enough to touch the pins.

  • Key A — brass, worn
  • Key B — silver, newer cut
  • Key C — unidentified, blackened metal
Status
Unclaimed. Under investigation.
