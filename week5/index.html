The Night the Harbor Bell Stopped

Six clues, one silence.

The mystery

The bell that didn't ring

Every night for forty years the harbor bell has marked midnight, and every night someone in the village has woken to it. Last night the rope was cut, the stairs were salted, and two cups sat on a table meant for one. The keeper swears he rang it and wrote it in his log. The only person awake to hear it says he did not. Someone is wrong.

Found things you can see

Torn rope key clue 4

The bell rope hangs frayed a hand's width below the beam. The ends are clean on one side and ragged on the other, as if it was cut and then pulled.

Salt on the stairs 2

A white trail leads up the tower steps, slightly damp near the top.

Two cups 3

One cold and empty, one warm and half full, both sat on the keeper's table.

Keeper's log 12 hold it to the light

"Midnight. Bell rung. All quiet." Faintly visible beneath the ink: "11:40. Rope cut. Boat at the stairs." The ink is neat, but the line sits slightly off from the rest of the page.

Told things you have heard

Keeper's statement 134

"I rang it. I always ring it. Nobody climbed those stairs but me, and I was alone all night."

Harbormaster on the tide

High water was at 11:40, he says. By midnight the harbor was already falling.

Neighbor's statement 1

"I wake for that bell every night. Last night I woke to nothing."

Fisherman's account 2

"Near a quarter to twelve I saw a lantern past the breakwater. Small boat, rowing away, no running light."

Investigator's notes

Contradictions

  1. The log says the bell rang at midnight. The neighbor heard nothing.
  2. The salt trail suggests a visitor from the water, but the log records no visitors.
  3. Two cups on the table, yet the keeper says he spent the night alone.
  4. A cut rope cannot ring a bell, yet the keeper swears he rang it.

Open questions

  • Who was the second cup for?
  • Who cut the rope, and why pull it afterward?
  • Why does the midnight line look separate from the rest?
  • Who rowed away from the stairs, and where did they land?

Next steps

  • Ask the keeper about the salt.
  • Compare the ink in the log entries.
  • Check the tide times with the harbormaster.
  • Find the fisherman and ask who was in the boat.
